Close relationships

Parenting

Parenting is regulation in the presence of someone else’s dysregulation — with the added weight that this person learned their nervous system from yours. The hardest part is that your child will find every unhealed wound you carry, without meaning to.

What makes this context different

A child’s dysregulation is one of the most powerful nervous system activators in existence. Their cry, their rage, their despair — all of it lands in the parent’s body as signal. The parent who has not processed their own early pain will find their child’s emotions triggering their own archived states.

Van der Kolk: “The body keeps the score.” In parenting, the body that keeps the score is often reliving an old script — and playing it out with a new cast.

Key lens for this context

Van der Kolk on intergenerational trauma. Bradshaw on the wounded inner child as the hidden third party in parenting. Porges on co-regulation as the core mechanism of early attachment. Schwartz on parenting from Self vs. from a part.

Patterns that show up here
Emotional flooding / reactivity
Discharge Move toward
The logic: The child’s emotion activates my own archived pain; what comes out is the old feeling, not the present one.
The cost: The child learns that their emotions are dangerous; they begin to manage themselves for the parent.
Resistance: If I slow down enough to feel what’s happening, I may have to acknowledge how much I’m bringing from the past.
What it protects: Unprocessed grief or rage from my own childhood that was never witnessed.
A way through: The pause. One breath before responding. “This is my stuff, not theirs.”
Emotional shutdown / checking out
Numb Move away
The logic: Disconnecting from the child’s need is the only way to keep functioning.
The cost: The child learns that emotional need leads to emptiness. They begin to stop bringing it.
Resistance: If I stay present for their pain, I’ll have to feel mine.
What it protects: Despair about one’s own capacity to give what one never received.
A way through: Repair after shutdown is more powerful than perfect presence. “I wasn’t here earlier. I’m here now.”
Over-controlling / anxious management
Control Move toward
The logic: If I manage every variable, my child won’t suffer what I suffered.
The cost: The child doesn’t develop tolerance for frustration or the experience of navigating difficulty with support.
Resistance: Letting go means accepting that I cannot protect them — and I carry enormous pain about not having been protected.
What it protects: The parent’s own unprocessed helplessness.
A way through: Tolerate small doses of the child’s distress. Stay close without fixing. Presence, not management.
Parentification / role reversal
Move toward Control
The logic: My child is unusually mature, perceptive, or capable of holding my feelings.
The cost: The child develops a self organized around the parent’s regulation, not their own development.
Resistance: If I stop leaning on my child, I’ll have to face how alone I actually am.
What it protects: The parent’s isolation and unmet need for support from adult peers.
A way through: Build an adult support system. The child’s job is to be a child.
