Self & internal world

With yourself

The relationship with the self is the template for all the others. The inner critic, self-abandonment, the gap between what you need and what you allow yourself to have. This is where the armoring begins.

What makes this context different

In most relational contexts, there’s another person whose behavior shapes the cycle. With yourself, there is no outside party. The loop runs faster, quieter, and without witness. The defenses that protect you from others often turn inward first.

These strategies tend to feel like personality rather than pattern. Intellectualizing feels like being smart. Self-abandonment feels like being accommodating. Numbing feels like being fine. The work is learning to read these as moves: the same six moves, turned inward.

Key lens for this context

IFS (Internal Family Systems): the self contains multiple parts, some of whom manage by controlling, some by numbing, some by protesting. John Bradshaw’s work on the wounded inner child. Orna Guralnik on the self as a relational field.

Patterns that show up here
Inner critic / self-attack
Discharge Control
The logic: If I criticize myself first, I won’t be caught off guard by the criticism from outside.
The cost: Chronic shame. The inner critic gets louder than any external voice.
Resistance: If I stop the self-attack, I might become complacent, fail, or expose myself to real judgment.
What it protects: The grief of not having been seen and accepted as you actually were.
A way through: Learn to hear the critic as a part: scared, not malicious. Ask it what it’s afraid will happen if it lets up.
Self-abandonment
Move away Numb
The logic: My needs are too much. Better to disappear them before they cause a problem.
The cost: A life arranged around everyone else’s needs. Resentment with no clear target.
Resistance: If I put myself first, I’ll be seen as selfish, or discovered to be the difficult one I always feared I was.
What it protects: The childhood belief that love is conditional on smallness.
A way through: One small act of self-regard per day. Not as indulgence, but as repair.
Numbing / dissociation
Numb Move away
The logic: Disconnecting from the body and feelings is the only way to keep functioning.
The cost: Presence goes offline. Decisions feel arbitrary. Meaning fades.
Resistance: What’s underneath the numbness may be too large to survive feeling.
What it protects: Unprocessed grief, rage, or terror from an earlier time.
A way through: Re-enter feeling through the body: slow movement, sensation, breath. Feeling in small doses, before it has to arrive all at once.
Perfectionism
Control Move away
The logic: If it’s perfect, it can’t be criticized. If it’s never finished, it can never fail.
The cost: Nothing ships. Nothing gets to be real. The gap between the ideal and the actual becomes intolerable.
Resistance: Done and imperfect means exposed. The work becomes proof of inadequacy.
What it protects: The shame of a self that was never allowed to be enough as-is.
A way through: Deliberately produce something imperfect and survive it. The recovery from imperfection is the practice.
Chronic busyness / self-neglect
Control Numb
The logic: If I’m always doing, I don’t have to feel what happens when I stop.
The cost: The body collapses eventually. The unlived life accumulates.
Resistance: Rest feels like failure. Stillness surfaces everything being outrun.
What it protects: The grief of a self that never learned it was allowed to simply exist.
A way through: Rest as resistance: not earned through productivity, but reclaimed as a right.
Intellectualizing your own pain
Control Numb
The logic: If I understand what’s happening, I can manage it without having to feel it.
The cost: Insight without integration. You know exactly why you are the way you are, and nothing changes.
Resistance: If I leave the mind and enter the feeling, I might lose control or look foolish.
What it protects: The raw need for comfort that the intellectual self has learned to shame.
A way through: One embodied moment before the analysis. What does this feel like in the chest, the throat, the gut?
